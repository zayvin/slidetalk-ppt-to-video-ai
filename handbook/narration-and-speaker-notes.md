# Turn speaker notes into a voiceover people can follow

Speaker notes may be just enough to remind a presenter what to say. A listener at home cannot see the presenter point at a bar, pause for a question or explain what a number measures. Keep the original notes open beside the slide. Draft the words the listener needs to hear, in the order the evidence appears.

## Before and after

Imagine a training slide that compares the **median time to finish one form**. The chart gives 12 minutes for the old form and 9 for the revised form. The presenter’s note says “Time down 25%. Mention pilot.”

> In our pilot, the median completion time was twelve minutes with the old form and nine with the revised form. That is a drop of three minutes, or a quarter of the original median.

“Median” matters here. If the audience is new to statistics, give the idea a sentence of its own.

> Line up the completion times from fastest to slowest and look at the middle. In our pilot, that time fell from twelve minutes to nine.

The second version gives the listener one comparison to hold in mind. Keep the sample description or dates on the slide if readers need them. If you do not know why the time changed, end the sentence after the observed change.

## A per-slide drafting pass

1. **Write the question in the margin.** “Did the revised form take less time?” gives the paragraph a clear end point.
2. **Name the visual evidence.** Replace “as you can see here” with an object or a label the listener can find, such as “the bar for the revised form.”
3. **Write for the ear.** Try “from twelve minutes to nine” in place of “12 → 9 min.” Read abbreviations aloud and supply a unit the first time the listener needs it.
4. **Read once while looking away.** Ask someone to repeat the comparison. If they remember “down three” but cannot say three *what*, move the unit closer to the number.

You can use these steps to write a script by hand or to edit an AI-generated draft. With SlideTalk, review the script one page at a time and audition a difficult sentence before generating the rest of the voiceover.
